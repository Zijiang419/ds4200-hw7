THE PROJECT / 01

AI use, in context.

A first look at how a synthetic student dataset is distributed across four AI-use bands.

A starting point, not a final answer

The group project examines how generative AI use relates to academic performance, study habits, skill retention, and well-being. Our starting dataset, Impact of AI on Students, contains 50,000 observations and 16 attributes. We created a reproducible sample of 8,000 rows by sampling within major and burnout-risk groups. We retained ten original variables and added three derived features: GPA change, total study hours, and an AI-use band. The cleaned sample has thirteen features.

The chart below counts the number of records in each use band. The categories are based on weekly AI-use hours and are shown in increasing order. The moderate band is the largest in this sample. These counts are a useful overview before comparing outcomes within groups; they are not a measure of how common these behaviors are among actual students. Hover over or focus on a bar to see its exact count. A reader can use the chart to ask whether later comparisons have similar numbers of records in each band.

What we plan to examine

  • Compare AI-use levels across academic majors.
  • Explore GPA change and skill retention alongside study habits.
  • Compare AI dependency and burnout risk across groups.

Several limits matter. This dataset is synthetic, and it does not establish a representative sample of any university. It also lacks information such as course difficulty and demographic context. Even if two variables move together, the chart cannot show that one caused the other. We will present results as exploratory patterns and explain uncertainty rather than making broad claims. The team will combine static comparisons with interactive views so readers can explore the evidence and see where the data may not answer a question.
