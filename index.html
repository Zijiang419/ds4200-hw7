ABOUT THIS SPACE

Questions before conclusions.

My name is Zijiang Yang. This site shares a topic I am exploring in DS4200: how students use generative AI and how those habits relate to learning. AI can help people get started, explain an unfamiliar idea, and organize a difficult task. At the same time, it raises questions about independent thinking, study time, and the way students feel about their work. I want to use data visualization to make those questions easier to discuss.

A useful chart should do more than look attractive. It should make a question clear, show where the data comes from, and help a reader notice both patterns and exceptions. That means selecting meaningful variables, choosing readable labels, and thinking carefully about what a comparison can and cannot show. I am especially interested in using simple interactive tools to let readers explore a group or value without losing the wider context.

The project presented here uses a cleaned sample of a dataset about AI use, study practices, academic outcomes, and burnout risk. The data is synthetic, so it should not be treated as a real student survey or proof that AI causes any outcome. Instead, it gives us a setting for practicing careful analysis and visualization design. The next page introduces the data and shows how the sample is divided into AI-use bands. This is an early step in a larger group project with Le Pham and Dan Chuyu. Our goal is to connect a sequence of charts into a clear, honest story about learning with AI.
